About

I think about software the way a city planner thinks about infrastructure. The interesting problem is rarely the single building: it's the roads, pipes and permits that let a thousand other people build without asking me first.

That's the thread through most of my work: AI-powered enterprise agents, microfrontend platforms serving thousands of requests a second, and design systems adopted across a dozen-plus teams. Different problems, same instinct: reduce the surface area a team has to think about so they can move faster on what actually matters.

I care about developer experience as much as user experience, about performance budgets as much as feature velocity, and about writing systems that are still legible to whoever inherits them in two years. Lately that means spending most of my time at the intersection of agentic AI and enterprise platform engineering: teaching large systems to do useful, permission-aware work without turning into a black box.
